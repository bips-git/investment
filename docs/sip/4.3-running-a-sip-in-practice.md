# Running a SIP in Practice

<Badge type="tip" text="Beginner" /> <Badge type="info" text="8 min read" />

::: info 🎯 What you will learn
- How a SIP is actually authorised through e-mandate or UPI AutoPay
- How to step up, pause or stop a SIP
- What happens when a SIP installment fails
- What cut-off times mean for NAV allotment
:::

## In plain words

Think of a school fee auto-debit arranged once with a bank, so fees are paid automatically
every month without needing to visit the school each time. Running a SIP works the same
way: you authorise it once, and then it runs automatically, following the money trail
described in Unit 2.2, until you change or stop it.

## How it works

### e-mandate and UPI AutoPay

**e-mandate** - an electronic authorisation given to a bank or platform, permitting
automatic debit of a fixed amount from a bank account, at set intervals, without needing a
fresh approval each time. **UPI AutoPay** - a similar standing-instruction facility built
on UPI (Unified Payments Interface), commonly used for smaller, recurring SIP amounts.

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1 | You authorise an e-mandate or UPI AutoPay once, when setting up the SIP |
| 2 | On each SIP date, the authorised amount is automatically debited |
| 3 | The amount reaches the scheme's bank account, and units are allotted per Unit 4.1 |
| 4 | The mandate remains active until you pause, stop, or it expires, per your original
  authorisation |

### Step-up SIP

**Step-up SIP** - a facility to automatically increase the SIP amount by a fixed
percentage or amount, at regular intervals (commonly yearly), often used to increase
investments in line with rising income over time.

### Pausing and stopping a SIP

| Action | What it does | Typical effect |
|---|---|---|
| Pause | Temporarily stops installments for a defined period | SIP resumes automatically after the pause period, if supported by the scheme |
| Stop | Permanently ends the SIP mandate | No further installments are debited, unless a new SIP is set up |

::: warning 📅 Check the latest
Whether a specific pause facility is available, and for how long, varies by AMC and
platform. Confirm the current pause and stop process, and any notice period required, on
the relevant AMC's or platform's website.
:::

### Failed installments

A SIP installment can fail for reasons such as insufficient bank balance, an expired
mandate, or a bank-side technical issue.

1. Check whether the mandate is still active and correctly linked to your current bank
   account.
2. Ensure sufficient balance is available in the account a day or two before the SIP date.
3. If an installment fails, that specific month's units are simply not allotted; future
   installments are usually unaffected, unless the mandate itself has failed or expired.
4. Repeated failures may lead the platform or bank to flag or deactivate the mandate,
   requiring it to be re-authorised.

### Cut-off times

**Cut-off time** - the specific time of day by which a mutual fund transaction request
must be received, to be processed at that same business day's NAV. Requests received after
the cut-off time are usually processed at the next business day's NAV instead.

| Scheme type | Typical cut-off time pattern |
|---|---|
| Equity and most other funds | Often around 3:00 PM on a business day |
| Liquid and overnight funds | Often earlier, sometimes with a same-day cut-off for the previous day's NAV |

::: warning 📅 Check the latest
Exact cut-off times can differ by scheme category and can change. Confirm the current
cut-off time for a specific scheme on the AMC's or RTA's website before relying on it for a
time-sensitive transaction.
:::

::: tip 💡 Real-life example
Nisha has a SIP of ₹4,000 per month, set up through UPI AutoPay.

| Event | What happens |
|---|---|
| SIP date, sufficient balance available | ₹4,000 is debited, and units are allotted at that day's NAV, if received before cut-off |
| SIP date, insufficient balance | The installment fails; that month's units are not allotted, but the mandate usually remains active for future months |
| One year later, income rises | Nisha activates a step-up SIP, increasing her installment to ₹4,400 |
| Nisha changes jobs, expects income disruption | She pauses the SIP for two months, then it resumes automatically |

This is illustrative, not a forecast. It shows how the mandate framework allows flexibility
without requiring a fresh SIP to be set up for every small change.
:::

::: warning ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Not maintaining sufficient balance around the SIP date, causing avoidable failed
  installments.
- Assuming a paused SIP has been permanently stopped, and being surprised when it resumes.
- Missing the cut-off time and assuming units were allotted at the intended day's NAV.
- Letting a mandate expire without renewing it, silently ending future installments.
:::

::: danger 🚨 Safety check
Only authorise e-mandates or UPI AutoPay through official bank or platform channels.
Never share UPI PINs or approve mandate requests you did not personally initiate,
including ones arriving through unexpected links or messages.
:::

::: details 🧠 Go deeper (university level)
NPCI (National Payments Corporation of India) governs the technical standards behind UPI
AutoPay, while e-mandates more broadly follow RBI's framework for recurring electronic
payment authorisations. AMFI investor education material describes step-up SIPs as a
practical tool to align ongoing investment amounts with rising income over a career,
supporting the long-term compounding effect discussed in Unit 1.2.
:::

## Quick recap

- e-mandate and UPI AutoPay let a fixed SIP amount be debited automatically, once
  authorised.
- Step-up SIPs increase the installment amount over time, often to match rising income.
- Pausing temporarily halts installments; stopping ends the mandate permanently.
- A failed installment usually means that month's units are not allotted, without
  affecting future installments, unless the mandate itself fails.
- Cut-off time determines which business day's NAV a transaction is processed at.
