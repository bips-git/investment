# Who's Who in Indian Markets

<Badge type="tip" text="Beginner" /> <Badge type="info" text="9 min read" />

::: info 🎯 What you will learn
- Who regulates Indian stock and mutual fund markets
- What stock exchanges and depositories actually do
- What clearing corporations, AMCs and trustees are for
- How all these pieces fit together when you invest
:::

## In plain words

Think of a big school. There is a principal who sets the rules (the regulator), classrooms
where lessons actually happen (the exchanges), a record room that keeps every student's
file safe (the depositories), and a set of supervisors making sure homework is fair and
graded correctly (clearing corporations and trustees). Indian markets work the same way:
several different institutions each play one clear role, so that your money and your
investments stay safe and properly recorded.

## How it works

### The regulator: SEBI

**SEBI** (Securities and Exchange Board of India) - the main regulator of India's stock
markets and mutual fund industry. SEBI makes rules to protect investors, registers market
participants such as brokers and mutual funds, and takes action against fraud and
malpractice.

### The central bank: RBI

**RBI** (Reserve Bank of India), already introduced in Unit 1.1, also regulates banks and
oversees India's overall monetary and payment systems, which markets depend on for
settlement and banking.

### The exchanges: NSE and BSE

**NSE** (National Stock Exchange) and **BSE** (Bombay Stock Exchange) are India's two
main stock exchanges - marketplaces where buyers and sellers meet to trade shares, bonds
and other listed instruments, under SEBI's supervision. Trading mechanics on exchanges are
covered in detail in Chapter 5.

### The depositories: NSDL and CDSL

**NSDL** (National Securities Depository Limited) and **CDSL** (Central Depository
Services Limited) are India's two depositories - institutions that hold shares and other
securities in electronic form, in **demat** (dematerialised) accounts, instead of paper
certificates. Demat accounts are covered further in Unit 2.3.

### Clearing corporations

**Clearing corporations** are institutions that guarantee and settle trades made on stock
exchanges. When you buy a share, a clearing corporation ensures the seller actually
delivers it, and you actually receive payment if you sell, even if the other party
defaults.

### Asset Management Companies and trustees

**AMC** (Asset Management Company) - the company that actually manages a mutual fund's
investments, such as choosing which shares or bonds to buy. **Trustees** are a separate
body that oversees the AMC on behalf of investors, checking that the fund is run in
investors' interest and in line with SEBI's rules. Mutual funds are covered in full in
Chapter 3.

### The industry body: AMFI

**AMFI** (Association of Mutual Funds in India) - the industry association of mutual fund
companies in India. AMFI runs investor awareness programmes, maintains distributor
registration, and publishes industry-wide mutual fund data.

### How the pieces connect

| Institution | Main role |
|---|---|
| SEBI | Regulator - sets rules, protects investors, supervises everyone else in this table |
| RBI | Central bank - regulates banks and payment systems markets depend on |
| NSE / BSE | Exchanges - where shares and bonds are actually bought and sold |
| NSDL / CDSL | Depositories - hold securities electronically in demat accounts |
| Clearing corporations | Guarantee and settle trades between buyers and sellers |
| AMC | Manages a mutual fund's actual investments |
| Trustees | Oversee the AMC on behalf of investors |
| AMFI | Industry body for mutual funds, investor awareness and data |

::: tip 💡 Real-life example
Sunita buys ₹10,000 worth of a company's shares through her broker's app.

| Step | What happens | Institution involved |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sunita places a buy order | Broker (registered with SEBI) |
| 2 | Order is matched with a seller | NSE or BSE |
| 3 | Trade is guaranteed and settled | Clearing corporation |
| 4 | Shares are credited to Sunita's demat account | NSDL or CDSL, through her Depository Participant |

This is illustrative, not a forecast. Every step is supervised so that Sunita's shares and
money are accounted for correctly, even though several different institutions are involved
behind the scenes.
:::

::: warning ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Assuming a broker app itself is the regulator, exchange, or depository.
- Thinking NSE and BSE are the only bodies protecting investors, and forgetting SEBI's
  role in setting the rules they follow.
- Confusing an AMC (which manages a fund) with a trustee (which oversees the AMC).
- Not checking whether a broker, distributor or AMC is actually registered with SEBI.
:::

::: danger 🚨 Safety check
Always confirm that any broker, distributor, or AMC you deal with is registered with SEBI.
Unregistered entities offering to "manage your investments" or promising guaranteed
returns are a common source of fraud, covered further in Chapter 9.
:::

::: details 🧠 Go deeper (university level)
NISM (National Institute of Securities Markets) workbooks describe this structure as
India's market infrastructure, split into regulators (SEBI, RBI), market infrastructure
institutions (exchanges, depositories, clearing corporations), and market intermediaries
(brokers, AMCs, distributors). This separation of roles, sometimes called a system of
checks and balances, is designed so that no single institution controls both the rules and
the money flow, reducing the chance of fraud going undetected.
:::

## Quick recap

- SEBI regulates India's securities markets; RBI regulates banks and payment systems.
- NSE and BSE are exchanges where shares and bonds are actually traded.
- NSDL and CDSL are depositories that hold securities electronically in demat accounts.
- Clearing corporations guarantee that trades are actually settled.
- AMCs manage mutual fund investments, trustees oversee AMCs, and AMFI is the industry
  body for mutual funds.

## Check yourself

1. What is the main difference between SEBI's role and an exchange's role?
   ::: details Show answer
   SEBI is the regulator that sets rules and protects investors. An exchange, such as NSE
   or BSE, is the marketplace where buying and selling of shares and bonds actually happens,
   under SEBI's supervision.
   :::
2. What do NSDL and CDSL actually do?
   ::: details Show answer
   They are depositories that hold shares and other securities electronically in demat
   accounts, instead of paper certificates.
   :::
3. Why does a clearing corporation matter when you buy or sell shares?
   ::: details Show answer
   It guarantees that the trade is settled, ensuring the buyer receives the shares and the
   seller receives payment, even if the other party fails to deliver.
   :::
4. What is the difference between an AMC and a trustee in a mutual fund?
   ::: details Show answer
   The AMC actually manages the fund's investments. The trustee is a separate body that
   oversees the AMC to ensure it acts in investors' interest, following SEBI's rules.
   :::
