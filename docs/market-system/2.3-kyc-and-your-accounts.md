# KYC and Your Accounts

## In plain words

Think of enrolling in a new school. You first submit identity proof once at admission
(this is like KYC), and you get a single roll number (your identity), but you may still
have separate cards for the library, the lab, and the sports room, each used for a
different purpose. Investing works similarly: one identity check, but several different
accounts, each serving a different function.

### PAN

**PAN** (Permanent Account Number) - a ten-character identity number issued by the Income
Tax Department, required for almost all financial transactions in India, including
opening bank, demat and trading accounts, and investing in mutual funds.

### KYC and CKYC

**KYC** (Know Your Customer) - a one-time identity and address verification process
required before you can invest, involving documents like PAN and proof of address.
**CKYC** (Central KYC) - a shared system where your KYC details, once verified by one
financial institution, can be reused by others, reducing repeated paperwork.

::: warning 📅 Check the latest
KYC document requirements and the process for updating KYC change from time to time.
Confirm the current requirements on the CKYC Registry website or your bank's website
before submitting documents.
:::

## How it works

### Four different accounts, four different jobs

| Account type | What it is for | Who provides it |
|---|---|---|
| Bank account | Holds your cash, sends and receives payments | A bank |
| Demat account | Holds shares and other securities electronically, introduced in Unit 2.1 | A Depository Participant, linked to NSDL or CDSL |
| Trading account | Used to place buy and sell orders on an exchange | A stockbroker |
| Folio | Tracks your mutual fund units with a specific AMC, introduced in Unit 2.2 | An AMC, through its RTA |

A demat account and a trading account are usually opened together through the same
broker, but they serve different purposes: the trading account is used to place orders,
and the demat account is where the resulting shares are actually held.

Mutual fund investments do not require a demat account if bought directly through an AMC
or RTA-linked platform in "statement" form, though many investors also choose to hold
mutual fund units in demat form through their broker.

### Nominee

A **nominee** is a person named on an account or folio to receive its assets if the
account holder passes away. Adding a nominee does not affect the account holder's rights
during their lifetime, but it can make the process of transferring assets to the family
faster and simpler afterward.

### Statement of account vs demat holding statement

A **statement of account** (for mutual funds) shows folio details, transactions, and
units held, issued by the RTA. A **demat holding statement** shows the shares and other
securities held in a demat account, issued by the Depository Participant, and can be
cross-checked directly with NSDL or CDSL records.

| Document | Covers | Issued by |
|---|---|---|
| Statement of account | Mutual fund folio and units | RTA (KFintech or CAMS) |
| Demat holding statement | Shares and other securities in demat form | Depository Participant, linked to NSDL or CDSL |

::: tip 💡 Real-life example
Meera opens her first investing setup.

| Step | Account or document | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Completes KYC once with PAN and address proof | Verified centrally through CKYC |
| 2 | Opens a bank account | To hold cash and make payments |
| 3 | Opens a demat and trading account with a broker | To buy and hold shares |
| 4 | Invests in a mutual fund, receiving a folio number | To track her mutual fund units |
| 5 | Adds a nominee to her demat account and folio | So her family can claim assets smoothly later |

This is illustrative, not a forecast. Meera now has one verified identity (KYC), but four
distinct records: bank account, demat account, trading account, and folio.
:::

::: warning ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Assuming a demat account and a trading account are the same thing.
- Forgetting to add a nominee, which can make claiming assets slower for family members.
- Confusing a mutual fund statement of account with a demat holding statement.
- Not updating KYC details, such as address or mobile number, after they change.
:::

::: danger 🚨 Safety check
Never share your PAN, KYC documents, or account login details with anyone claiming to
"complete your KYC" over an unsolicited call or message. Complete or update KYC only
through official bank, broker, AMC, or CKYC Registry channels.
:::

::: details 🧠 Go deeper (university level)
SEBI's KYC framework, implemented through CKYC and periodic due diligence requirements, is
part of a broader anti-money-laundering and investor-protection structure, referenced in
NISM workbooks and SEBI circulars. The separation between a trading account (used to place
orders on the exchange) and a demat account (used to hold securities) reflects the same
principle of separating roles seen in Unit 2.1, where no single entity controls the entire
chain from order placement to custody of assets.
:::

## Quick recap

- PAN is a required identity number for financial transactions in India.
- KYC is a one-time identity check; CKYC allows it to be reused across institutions.
- Bank, demat and trading accounts each serve a different purpose; a folio tracks mutual
  fund units.
- A nominee helps ensure assets transfer smoothly to family if something happens to the
  account holder.
- A mutual fund statement of account and a demat holding statement are different documents
  from different issuers.

## Check yourself

1. What is the main difference between a demat account and a trading account?
   ::: details Show answer
   A trading account is used to place buy and sell orders on an exchange. A demat account
   is where the resulting shares are actually held in electronic form.
   :::
2. Why does CKYC reduce repeated paperwork?
   ::: details Show answer
   Once your KYC is verified by one institution and recorded centrally, other institutions
   can reuse the same verified KYC details instead of asking you to repeat the process.
   :::
3. Why should you add a nominee to your investment accounts?
   ::: details Show answer
   A nominee helps ensure that assets can be transferred to your family more smoothly if
   something happens to you, without changing your own rights over the account meanwhile.
   :::
4. If you want to confirm your mutual fund folio details, which document should you check?
   ::: details Show answer
   The statement of account, issued by the RTA (KFintech or CAMS), which shows folio
   details, transactions and units held.
   :::
