# What Apps Like Groww Really Are

## In plain words

Think of a travel booking app. It does not own the airline or the train, but it helps you
book a ticket, and it earns a fee or commission for doing so. Investing apps work in a
similar way: most of them do not manufacture the shares or mutual funds you buy. They are
the front door, or the selling channel, connecting you to the institutions covered in
Units 2.1 and 2.2.

## How it works

### Broker vs distributor vs aggregator

| Type | What it does | SEBI registration |
|---|---|---|
| Broker | Lets you buy and sell shares, bonds, and other listed instruments through an exchange | Registered as a stockbroker with SEBI |
| Distributor | Sells mutual fund schemes on behalf of AMCs, usually earning a commission | Registered with AMFI, following SEBI rules |
| Aggregator | Shows information, comparisons, or offers across multiple products or providers | May or may not itself be a registered broker or distributor |

Some apps combine more than one role. A single app might act as a broker for shares and
also as a distributor for mutual funds. It is worth checking, on the app or its website,
exactly which registrations it holds for each activity it offers.

### How these apps earn money

Apps generally earn through one or more of the following:

1. **Brokerage and fees** - charges for buying or selling shares, or for account
   maintenance.
2. **Commission from AMCs** - distributors earn a commission for selling regular plans of
   mutual funds, paid by the AMC, not directly by the investor.
3. **Interest on unused balances** - interest earned on client funds held briefly before
   investment or withdrawal.
4. **Other financial products** - some apps also earn by selling loans, insurance, or
   other financial products alongside investing services.

### Direct vs regular mutual fund plans

**Direct plan** - a mutual fund plan bought straight from the AMC or an RTA-linked
platform, without a distributor, so it carries a lower expense ratio (fund costs are
covered fully in Unit 3.3). **Regular plan** - a mutual fund plan bought through a
distributor, which includes a distributor's commission built into a higher expense ratio.

| Plan type | Who you buy through | Relative cost | Who earns the distribution commission |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct | AMC directly, or an RTA-linked platform | Lower expense ratio | No distributor commission |
| Regular | A distributor, including many apps | Higher expense ratio | The distributor |

::: warning 📅 Check the latest
Whether a specific app offers direct plans, regular plans, or both, and how it labels
this choice, can change over time. Always check the exact plan type shown at the time of
investing, on the order confirmation screen, before completing a purchase.
:::

### What a Groww-type app screen might show

A typical order screen for a mutual fund purchase might display: scheme name, NAV,
minimum investment amount, plan type (direct or regular), and expense ratio. This
description is illustrative, not a recommendation or endorsement of any specific app.
Always read this screen carefully before confirming an order.

### What an app can and cannot protect

| An app generally can | An app generally cannot |
|---|---|
| Show you SEBI-registered products and their disclosed details | Guarantee that any investment will not fall in value |
| Route your order safely to the exchange, AMC or RTA | Remove the market, credit or liquidity risk described in Unit 1.3 |
| Provide access to your official statements and holdings | Reverse a loss caused by market movements |
| Follow SEBI and RBI rules for handling your money and data | Compensate you for choices, such as risky products, that you chose yourself |

::: tip 💡 Real-life example
Vikram compares buying the same mutual fund scheme two ways.

| Detail | Direct plan | Regular plan |
|---|---|---|
| Bought through | AMC's own platform | A distributor app |
| Approximate expense ratio | 0.8% per year | 1.5% per year |
| Distributor commission | None | Built into the expense ratio |

Illustrative, not a forecast. Over many years, this 0.7 percentage point difference in
expense ratio can add up to a noticeably different final amount, purely due to costs, even
if both plans invest in the exact same underlying portfolio. The full cost impact over
time is covered in Unit 3.3.
:::

::: warning ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Assuming an app is itself a regulator, guaranteeing safety of the investment.
- Not noticing whether an order is being placed as a direct or a regular plan.
- Believing "the app is trustworthy" is the same as "the underlying investment is safe".
- Ignoring the actual registration type (broker, distributor, aggregator) an app holds for
  each product it offers.
:::

::: danger 🚨 Safety check
An app being popular or well-designed does not remove investment risk. Always verify an
app's SEBI or AMFI registration independently, and remember that no legitimate app, broker
or distributor can guarantee investment returns. Guaranteed-return claims are covered as a
major red flag in Chapter 9.
:::

::: details 🧠 Go deeper (university level)
SEBI's regulatory framework separates market intermediaries by function: brokers
(registered under SEBI's stockbroker regulations), mutual fund distributors (registered
under AMFI, following SEBI's mutual fund regulations), and investment advisers (a
separate SEBI registration for those charging fees for personalised advice). This
functional separation, referenced in NISM distributor workbooks, means an app's core
role, and its incentive structure, depends heavily on which of these registrations it
holds for a given product.
:::

## Quick recap

- Apps can act as brokers, distributors, aggregators, or a combination of these roles.
- Apps typically earn through brokerage, distributor commissions, interest, or other
  products.
- Direct plans skip the distributor and have a lower expense ratio; regular plans include
  a distributor's commission.
- An app can route orders and disclose registered information, but cannot remove market
  risk or guarantee returns.
- Checking an app's exact registration and plan type matters more than its popularity or
  design.

## Check yourself

1. What is the main difference between a broker and a mutual fund distributor?
   ::: details Show answer
   A broker lets you buy and sell shares and other listed instruments through an exchange.
   A distributor sells mutual fund schemes on behalf of AMCs, usually earning a commission
   from the AMC.
   :::
2. Why does a regular mutual fund plan usually have a higher expense ratio than a direct
   plan?
   ::: details Show answer
   A regular plan includes a distributor's commission built into its expense ratio, while a
   direct plan is bought without a distributor and does not carry that commission.
   :::
3. Can an investing app guarantee that your investment will not lose value?
   ::: details Show answer
   No. An app can route orders and disclose registered information, but it cannot remove
   market, credit or liquidity risk, or guarantee returns.
   :::
4. Why is it useful to check whether an app is registered as a broker, distributor, or
   both?
   ::: details Show answer
   The registration determines what activities the app is legally allowed to offer and how
   it earns money, which affects the fees or commission structure you may be paying.
   :::
