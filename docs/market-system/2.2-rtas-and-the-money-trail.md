# RTAs and the Money Trail

<Badge type="tip" text="Beginner" /> <Badge type="info" text="8 min read" />

::: info 🎯 What you will learn

- What KFintech and CAMS actually do

## In plain words

Think of a large school with thousands of students across many branches. The school itself
focuses on teaching, but it hires a separate record-keeping office to maintain admission
files, fee receipts and report cards for every student, across every branch. Mutual funds
work in a similar way. The AMC (introduced in Unit 2.1) focuses on managing investments,
while a separate specialist keeps the detailed records of every investor's units.

## How it works

### What an RTA is

**RTA** (Registrar and Transfer Agent) - a company that maintains investor records for
mutual funds, such as units held, transactions made, and personal details on file. An RTA
works on behalf of AMCs, but is a separate company from them.

### KFintech and CAMS

**KFintech** and **CAMS** (Computer Age Management Services) are India's two largest RTAs
for mutual funds. Between them, they handle record-keeping for most mutual fund AMCs in
India. When you buy or redeem mutual fund units, it is usually the RTA's systems that
process and record the transaction on the AMC's behalf.

### What a folio is

A **folio** (folio number) is a unique account number assigned to an investor by an RTA,
used to track all mutual fund investments made by that investor, across one or more
schemes of the same AMC. Think of it as a roll number: one folio can hold many different
mutual fund "subjects" (schemes) under the same student's (investor's) record.

### MFCentral

**MFCentral** is a shared online portal, set up under AMFI and RTA support, where an
investor can view mutual fund holdings across different AMCs and RTAs in one place, and
carry out certain service requests, such as updating contact details or nominee
information.

### The money trail: what happens when you tap "Invest"

1. You open an app (broker, distributor, or AMC's own app) and place a buy order for a
   mutual fund scheme.
2. The app forwards your order and payment instruction to the AMC, often through the RTA's
   systems.
3. Your payment moves from your bank account to the mutual fund scheme's bank account,
   usually through UPI or net banking.
4. The RTA (KFintech or CAMS) processes the order, and units are allotted at that day's
   **NAV** (Net Asset Value), covered fully in Unit 3.1.
5. The RTA updates your folio to reflect the new units, and a statement is generated.

| Step | Institution mainly responsible |
|---|---|
| Placing the order | The app (broker, distributor, or AMC) |
| Moving the payment | Banking system (UPI or net banking) |
| Recording units and folio | RTA (KFintech or CAMS) |
| Managing the underlying investments | AMC |

::: tip 💡 Real-life example
Rahul invests ₹5,000 in a mutual fund scheme through an app.

| Time | What happens |
|---|---|
| 10:00 AM | Rahul places the order in the app and pays via UPI |
| Same day | Payment reaches the scheme's bank account |
| Same or next business day | RTA processes the order and allots units at that day's NAV |
| Within a few days | Rahul receives an email or SMS statement showing his folio number and units |

All figures and timings are illustrative, not a forecast, and can vary by scheme, cut-off
time, and payment method. Cut-off times are covered further in Unit 4.3.
:::

::: warning ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Assuming the app you invest through is the same company that actually holds your
  records; it is often just the front end, with the RTA keeping the real records.
- Losing track of folio numbers across different AMCs, making it hard to consolidate
  holdings.
- Not checking MFCentral or RTA statements periodically to confirm holdings match what an
  app shows.
- Forgetting to update nominee or contact details with the RTA after a life change, such as
  marriage or a new address.
:::

::: danger 🚨 Safety check
Your official proof of mutual fund holdings is the RTA's statement (from KFintech, CAMS,
or MFCentral), not just what an app's screen shows. If an app ever shows something
inconsistent with your RTA statement, treat the RTA record as the authoritative one and
raise a query with the AMC or RTA directly.
:::

::: details 🧠 Go deeper (university level)
SEBI's regulatory framework requires every mutual fund to appoint a Registrar and Transfer
Agent to maintain investor records independently of the distribution channel, adding a
layer of separation between the front-end app or distributor and the official record of
ownership. This separation is part of SEBI's broader design of checks and balances in the
mutual fund industry, referenced in Unit 2.1, and is described in NISM's mutual fund
distributor workbooks.
:::

## Quick recap

- An RTA maintains investor records for mutual funds on behalf of AMCs.
- KFintech and CAMS are India's two largest mutual fund RTAs.
- A folio number is your unique account number with an AMC, tracked by its RTA.
- MFCentral lets you view holdings across AMCs and RTAs in one place.
- The money trail runs from your bank, through the scheme's account, to the RTA's records,
  which update your folio.

## Check yourself

1. What is the difference between an AMC and an RTA?
   ::: details Show answer
   The AMC manages the mutual fund's actual investments. The RTA maintains investor
   records, such as units held and transactions made, on behalf of the AMC.
   :::
2. What does a folio number represent?
   ::: details Show answer
   It is a unique account number assigned by an RTA to track an investor's holdings across
   one or more schemes of the same AMC.
   :::
3. If your app's screen and your RTA statement disagree on your mutual fund holdings, which
   should you treat as authoritative?
   ::: details Show answer
   The RTA statement, since RTAs like KFintech and CAMS maintain the official investor
   records on behalf of AMCs.
   :::
4. What is MFCentral used for?
   ::: details Show answer
   It is a shared portal where investors can view mutual fund holdings across different
   AMCs and RTAs in one place, and carry out certain service requests.
   :::
