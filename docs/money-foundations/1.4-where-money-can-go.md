# Where Money Can Go

<Badge type="tip" text="Beginner" /> <Badge type="info" text="9 min read" />

::: info 🎯 What you will learn
- The main places Indian households can put their money
- How each option compares on safety, liquidity and return
- What PPF and EPF are, and how they differ from a bank FD
- Why no single option is "best" for every goal
:::

## In plain words

Think of money like water. It can sit still in a tank (very safe, easy to access, but does
not grow much), or it can be sent to grow crops (more effort and some risk, but the
potential to multiply). Different investment options are simply different "containers"
for money, each with its own trade-off between safety, ease of access, and growth
potential.

## How it works

### The main options

**Bank FD (Fixed Deposit)** - a bank account where a lump sum is locked in for a fixed
period at a fixed interest rate, set by the bank.

**PPF (Public Provident Fund)** - a long-term government savings scheme with a fixed
tenure, tax benefits, and an interest rate set by the government, reviewed periodically.

**EPF (Employees' Provident Fund)** - a retirement savings scheme for salaried employees,
where both employee and employer contribute a fixed percentage of salary every month.

**Gold** - a physical or financial asset (jewellery, coins, or gold-linked instruments)
often used as a store of value and a hedge against inflation and currency weakness.

**Debt** - lending money to a borrower, such as the government or a company, in exchange
for regular interest and return of principal at maturity. Bonds and debt mutual funds,
covered in Chapter 3, fall in this category.

**Equity** - buying a share of ownership in a company, covered in detail in Chapter 5.
Returns come from the company's growth and profit, and are not fixed or guaranteed.

**Real estate** - buying physical property such as land or a flat, for rental income,
capital appreciation, or personal use.

### Comparing on safety, liquidity and return

| Option | Safety | Liquidity | Typical return pattern |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bank FD | High | Moderate (penalty for early withdrawal) | Fixed, low to moderate |
| PPF | High (government-backed) | Low (long lock-in) | Fixed, moderate, tax-free |
| EPF | High (government-regulated) | Low (mainly on retirement or job change) | Fixed, moderate, tax-free within limits |
| Gold | Moderate (price can be volatile) | High (can usually be sold quickly) | Variable, tends to track inflation over long periods |
| Debt (bonds, debt funds) | Varies by issuer (see credit risk, Unit 1.3) | Moderate to high | Fixed or near-fixed, low to moderate |
| Equity (shares, equity funds) | Lower (higher volatility) | High (for listed shares) | Variable, potentially high over long periods, not guaranteed |
| Real estate | Moderate (depends on location and demand) | Low (can take months to sell) | Variable, includes rental income and price change |

This table shows general, typical patterns. It is illustrative, not a forecast, and actual
outcomes vary by specific product, issuer, and market conditions.

::: warning 📅 Check the latest
Interest rates for bank FDs, PPF and EPF change from time to time, and PPF and EPF rates
are set or reviewed by the government. Confirm current rates on your bank's website, the
National Savings Institute or India Post website for PPF, and the EPFO (Employees'
Provident Fund Organisation) website for EPF, before making any decision.
:::

### Matching options to goals

1. List each financial goal, such as an emergency fund, a child's education, or retirement.
2. Note how many years away each goal is.
3. Match short-term goals (under 3 years) to safer, more liquid options like a bank FD or
   liquid fund.
4. Match long-term goals (10 years or more) to a mix that can include equity, since there
   is more time to ride out its volatility, discussed in Unit 1.3.
5. Use PPF and EPF mainly for long-term, retirement-linked goals, given their long
   lock-in.

::: tip 💡 Real-life example
Suresh has ₹6,00,000 to place across different goals.

| Goal | Years away | Amount | Suggested type of option (illustrative) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Emergency fund | Immediate | ₹1,50,000 | Bank FD or liquid fund, for safety and easy access |
| Daughter's college fees | 8 years | ₹2,50,000 | A mix of debt and equity, for moderate growth with some safety |
| Retirement | 25 years | ₹2,00,000 | Mostly equity, plus PPF or EPF contributions, for long-term growth |

This allocation is illustrative, not a forecast or a recommendation for any specific
product. It only shows how time horizon can guide the choice of option.
:::

::: warning ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Putting emergency fund money into low-liquidity options like real estate or long-tenure
  schemes.
- Chasing gold or equity for a goal that is only a year or two away.
- Ignoring PPF and EPF's tax benefits when planning long-term, tax-efficient savings.
- Treating real estate as automatically safe, while ignoring its low liquidity and
  maintenance costs.
:::

::: danger 🚨 Safety check
Some unregistered schemes promise FD-like safety with much higher returns than banks or
PPF offer. This mismatch between promised safety and promised return is a common red flag,
covered further in Chapter 9. Genuine government-backed options like PPF and EPF are only
offered through official channels such as banks, India Post, or the EPFO portal.
:::

::: details 🧠 Go deeper (university level)
Prasanna Chandra's *Investment Analysis and Portfolio Management* frames this comparison
as choosing along three dimensions: safety of principal, liquidity (ease of converting to
cash without loss of value), and return (income plus capital appreciation). Modern
portfolio theory suggests combining options with different risk and return patterns,
rather than choosing only one, to manage overall portfolio risk. This idea of combining
options is developed further in Chapter 7 under asset allocation and diversification.
:::

## Quick recap

- Bank FD, PPF, EPF, gold, debt, equity and real estate are the main places money can go.
- Each option has its own trade-off between safety, liquidity and return.
- PPF and EPF are long-term, government-linked options with tax benefits and long lock-ins.
- Short-term goals suit safer, more liquid options; long-term goals can include more
  equity.
- No single option fits every goal; the right mix depends on time horizon and purpose.

## Check yourself

1. Why might real estate be a poor choice for an emergency fund?
   ::: details Show answer
   Real estate has low liquidity. It can take months to sell at a fair price, so it is not
   suitable for money that may be needed quickly in an emergency.
   :::
2. What is the key difference between PPF and a bank FD?
   ::: details Show answer
   PPF is a long-term, government-backed scheme with a long lock-in and tax benefits. A bank
   FD is more flexible in tenure and offered by banks, generally with shorter lock-in options
   and different tax treatment.
   :::
3. Why might a 25-year retirement goal reasonably include more equity than a 1-year goal?
   ::: details Show answer
   Equity is more volatile in the short term but has more time to recover and grow over a
   long horizon. A 1-year goal has little time to recover from a downturn, so it usually
   suits safer, more liquid options.
   :::
4. What is a common warning sign when a scheme claims FD-like safety with unusually high
   returns?
   ::: details Show answer
   This is a mismatch between promised safety and promised return, since the risk-return
   trade-off suggests higher returns usually carry higher risk. It is a common sign of a
   scam.
   :::
