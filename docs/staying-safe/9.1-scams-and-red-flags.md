# Scams and Red Flags

<Badge type="tip" text="Beginner" /> <Badge type="info" text="8 min read" />

::: info 🎯 What you will learn
- Why guaranteed-return promises are a major warning sign
- How fake apps and tip groups typically operate
- Why unregistered advisors are risky, even when they seem knowledgeable
- How to check SEBI registration before trusting any platform or advisor
:::

## In plain words

Think of a stranger promising to double your money in a month, with "zero risk". Just as
you would question a stranger's promise of guaranteed prize money for a simple task,
similar promises about investments deserve the same healthy scepticism. Scams often use
urgency, secrecy, and the promise of unusually high, guaranteed returns to bypass careful
thinking.

## How it works

### Guaranteed returns

As established through the risk-return trade-off in Unit 1.3, every investment carries
some risk, and returns are never truly guaranteed in equity, mutual funds, or most
securities markets. Any offer promising a guaranteed high return, especially one clearly
above what safe options like bank FDs or PPF (introduced in Unit 1.4) offer, is a major
red flag.

### Fake apps

**Fake apps** - fraudulent applications designed to look like a legitimate broker,
distributor, or investment platform, but which are not actually registered with SEBI, and
are designed to collect money or personal information without providing genuine
investment services.

1. Check that an app is linked to a SEBI-registered broker or AMFI-registered distributor,
   introduced in Unit 2.4, before depositing any money.
2. Verify the app through official channels, such as the SEBI or AMFI website, rather than
   trusting app store ratings or links shared through messages alone.
3. Be cautious of apps promoted heavily through unsolicited messages, promising unusually
   high or guaranteed returns.

### Tip groups and unregistered advisors

**Tip groups** - informal groups, often on messaging platforms, sharing stock "tips" or
recommendations, frequently without any SEBI registration or accountability for the advice
given. **Unregistered advisor** - a person or entity offering investment advice for a fee
or otherwise, without holding a valid SEBI Investment Adviser or Research Analyst
registration, where such registration is legally required.

| Red flag | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| "Guaranteed" or "sure-shot" tips | No legitimate market participant can guarantee specific stock outcomes |
| Pressure to act immediately | Urgency is often used to prevent careful, independent verification |
| Requests to trade through an unfamiliar or unverified platform | Bypasses your own broker's SEBI-registered systems |
| Reluctance to disclose SEBI registration details | Legitimate advisors are required to be transparent about their registration |

### Checking SEBI registration

1. Visit SEBI's official website and use its registered intermediaries search facility.
2. Search for the broker, distributor, investment adviser, or research analyst by name or
   registration number.
3. Confirm the registration is currently valid and matches the entity you are dealing
   with.
4. Similarly, check AMFI's website for a mutual fund distributor's registration status.

::: warning 📅 Check the latest
The exact process and website links for checking SEBI or AMFI registration can be
updated over time. Search for "SEBI registered intermediaries" or "AMFI registered
distributor check" on the official SEBI and AMFI websites for the current verification
tool.
:::

::: tip 💡 Real-life example
Priyanka receives a message in a group promising 5% guaranteed monthly returns through a
new "exclusive" trading app.

| Step Priyanka takes | Finding |
|---|---|
| Checks SEBI's registered intermediaries list for the app's claimed broker name | No matching registration found |
| Searches the app's name alongside "scam" or "fraud" using an independent web search | Finds warnings from other users |
| Declines to deposit money and reports the group | Avoids potential financial loss |

This is illustrative, not a forecast. It shows the kind of independent verification steps
that can help avoid a scam before money is lost.
:::

::: warning ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Trusting a platform or advisor purely based on confident-sounding claims or professional-
  looking marketing.
- Depositing money into an app or account before verifying its SEBI or AMFI registration.
- Believing that "everyone in the group is doing well" is proof of legitimacy, rather than
  a herding effect, introduced in Unit 7.4.
- Sharing OTPs, passwords, or screen-sharing access with anyone claiming to help "activate"
  an account or investment.
:::

::: danger 🚨 Safety check
Never share OTPs, passwords, or screen-sharing access with anyone, regardless of how
official they sound or how urgent the request seems. No legitimate SEBI-registered
broker, AMC, or bank will ever ask for these details over a call, message, or unsolicited
visit. If in doubt, independently verify registration and hang up or stop responding
immediately.
:::

::: details 🧠 Go deeper (university level)
SEBI's investor education material and periodic public advisories describe common scam
patterns, including "pump and dump" schemes (where a stock's price is artificially
inflated through coordinated tips, then sold off by the promoters, leaving later buyers
with losses) and fraudulent investment advisory schemes. NISM workbooks emphasise
independent verification of registration status as the single most reliable first check
before engaging with any investment platform or advisor, since registration status can be
confirmed directly through SEBI's own systems, independent of any claim made by the entity
itself.
:::

## Quick recap

- Guaranteed high returns with no risk are a major warning sign; no legitimate investment
  can promise this.
- Fake apps and unregistered advisors mimic legitimate services but lack accountability
  and proper registration.
- Tip groups promising sure-shot gains often use urgency and herding to bypass careful
  thinking.
- Always independently verify SEBI or AMFI registration before trusting any platform or
  advisor with your money.

## Check yourself

1. Why is a promise of "guaranteed" high returns a major red flag?
   ::: details Show answer
   Because every investment carries some risk, and legitimate market participants cannot
   guarantee specific returns, as established by the risk-return trade-off.
   :::
2. What is the most reliable first step to verify a broker, distributor, or advisor's
   legitimacy?
   ::: details Show answer
   Independently checking their registration status directly on SEBI's or AMFI's official
   website, rather than relying on their own claims or marketing.
   :::
3. Why should you never share an OTP with someone claiming to help you, even if they sound
   official?
   ::: details Show answer
   No legitimate broker, AMC, or bank will ever ask for your OTP; sharing it can allow
   unauthorised access to your accounts or funds.
   :::
4. Why can "everyone in the group is profiting" be a misleading signal?
   ::: details Show answer
   It can reflect a herding effect or a deliberately staged appearance of success, rather
   than genuine, verified evidence of legitimate returns.
   :::
