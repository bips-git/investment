# Safety Checklist for Any App

<Badge type="tip" text="Beginner" /> <Badge type="info" text="8 min read" />

::: info 🎯 What you will learn
- A practical checklist to verify any investing app before use
- Why 2FA matters, and how to use it properly
- Good OTP hygiene habits
- How to verify your actual holdings independently of any single app
:::

## In plain words

Think of checking that a school van's driver, route, and safety equipment are all in
order before letting your child board, rather than assuming everything is fine just
because the van looks well-maintained. Using an investing app deserves the same habit of
active, ongoing verification, not just a one-time check.

## How it works

### A practical safety checklist

| Check | What to confirm |
|---|---|
| Registration | The app is linked to a SEBI-registered broker or AMFI-registered distributor, verified independently as covered in Unit 9.1 |
| App source | Downloaded only from the official app store listing or the broker's official website, not from a link shared in a message |
| 2FA enabled | Two-factor authentication is turned on for login, discussed further below |
| OTP hygiene | OTPs are never shared with anyone, discussed further below |
| Independent holdings check | Holdings are periodically cross-checked against RTA and depository statements, discussed further below |
| Contact details updated | Registered mobile number and email are current, so alerts and OTPs reach you promptly |
| Nominee added | A nominee is set on demat and mutual fund accounts, as covered in Unit 2.3 |

### 2FA

**2FA** (Two-Factor Authentication) - a login security method requiring two separate forms
of verification, typically a password and a one-time code sent to your registered mobile
number or authentication app, making it harder for an unauthorised person to access your
account even if they know your password.

1. Enable 2FA wherever an app or platform offers it, rather than relying on a password
   alone.
2. Prefer an authentication app or biometric method where available, as an additional
   layer beyond SMS-based codes.
3. Never disable 2FA for convenience, even temporarily.

### OTP hygiene

**OTP** (One-Time Password) - a temporary code sent to verify a specific transaction or
login, intended to be used only by the account holder, for that specific action.

| Good OTP habit | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Never share an OTP with anyone, including someone claiming to be from your bank or broker | Legitimate institutions never ask for your OTP |
| Check the transaction detail shown alongside the OTP before entering it | Confirms the OTP matches the action you actually intended |
| Report immediately if you receive an OTP for a transaction you did not initiate | May indicate someone else is attempting unauthorised access |

### Verifying holdings independently

Relying on a single app's displayed holdings alone is not sufficient. As introduced in
Unit 2.2, the RTA's statement of account is the authoritative record for mutual fund
holdings, and the depository (NSDL or CDSL, Unit 2.1) holding statement is authoritative
for demat holdings.

1. Periodically download or request a statement directly from the RTA (KFintech, CAMS, or
   via MFCentral) for mutual fund holdings.
2. Periodically check your demat holding statement directly through NSDL's or CDSL's own
   portal, or your Depository Participant.
3. Compare these independent statements against what your everyday app shows, and
   investigate promptly if there is a mismatch.

::: warning 📅 Check the latest
Exact steps to access RTA or depository statements can change as platforms update their
websites and portals. Search for "NSDL holding statement" or "CDSL holding statement", and
"MFCentral" or your RTA's name, for the current official access process.
:::

::: tip 💡 Real-life example
Rajesh follows a personal safety routine every few months.

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Confirms his broker app still matches the SEBI registration he originally verified |
| 2 | Confirms 2FA is still enabled on all his investing apps |
| 3 | Downloads his latest demat holding statement directly from his Depository
    Participant's portal |
| 4 | Downloads his mutual fund statement of account via MFCentral |
| 5 | Compares both against what his everyday app shows, confirming everything matches |

This is illustrative, not a forecast. It shows a practical, repeatable routine for
ongoing account safety, rather than a one-time setup task.
:::

::: warning ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Downloading an investing app from a link shared in a message, instead of an official
  app store or website.
- Leaving 2FA disabled, or using an easily guessable password, for convenience.
- Sharing an OTP under pressure or urgency, even briefly.
- Never independently checking holdings against RTA or depository statements, relying
  solely on one app's display.
:::

::: danger 🚨 Safety check
If you ever notice a discrepancy between an app's displayed holdings and your official RTA
or depository statement, treat it seriously and raise it immediately with the AMC, broker,
or Depository Participant, and consider the complaint process covered in Unit 9.3 if not
resolved promptly.
:::

::: details 🧠 Go deeper (university level)
SEBI's cybersecurity and cyber resilience framework for market intermediaries mandates
specific security controls, including authentication requirements, for platforms handling
investor transactions. NISM investor education material emphasises that ultimate
responsibility for verifying holdings rests with the investor, since the RTA and
depository records, not any single app's interface, form the legally authoritative record
of ownership, referenced earlier in Unit 2.2.
:::

## Quick recap

- Verify an app's SEBI or AMFI registration and download it only from official sources.
- Enable 2FA on every investing app or platform, and never disable it for convenience.
- Never share an OTP with anyone, and check transaction details before entering one.
- Periodically verify your actual holdings independently, using RTA and depository
  statements, not just a single app's display.

## Check yourself

1. Why does 2FA add meaningful security beyond a password alone?
   ::: details Show answer
   It requires a second, separate form of verification, making it harder for an
   unauthorised person to access your account even if they know your password.
   :::
2. Why should you never share an OTP, even with someone claiming to be from your bank or
   broker?
   ::: details Show answer
   Legitimate institutions never ask for your OTP; sharing it can enable unauthorised access
   to your account or funds.
   :::
3. Which records are authoritative for checking your actual mutual fund and demat
   holdings?
   ::: details Show answer
   The RTA's statement of account for mutual funds, and the depository's (NSDL or CDSL)
   holding statement for demat holdings, rather than any single app's display alone.
   :::
4. What should you do if you receive an OTP for a transaction you did not initiate?
   ::: details Show answer
   Do not share or use it, and report it immediately to your bank or broker, since it may
   indicate someone else is attempting unauthorised access.
   :::
