# Leverage and Margin

<Badge type="tip" text="Beginner" /> <Badge type="info" text="8 min read" />

::: info 🎯 What you will learn
- What leverage means and how it magnifies both gains and losses
- What margin is, in this context
- What a margin call is
- What auto square-off means, and why it can happen without warning
:::

## In plain words

Think of using a small down payment to control a much larger asset, like paying a small
booking amount to reserve a flat worth far more. If the flat's value rises, your gain on
the small amount you actually paid can be very large in percentage terms. But if the value
falls, your loss can also be very large relative to what you put in. This is the basic idea
behind **leverage**.

## How it works

### What leverage means

**Leverage** - using borrowed money or a smaller upfront deposit to control a position
larger than the cash you have actually put in, which magnifies both potential gains and
potential losses, in percentage terms, compared to the amount you actually invested.

### Margin

**Margin** - the amount of money or securities you must deposit with a broker, as
collateral, to open and maintain a leveraged position, such as intraday trading (Unit
5.2) or the derivatives covered later in this chapter.

### A simple worked example of magnification

| Scenario | Own money used | Position size | Price move | Gain or loss on own money |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Without leverage | ₹1,00,000 | ₹1,00,000 | +5% | +₹5,000 (+5%) |
| With 5x leverage | ₹1,00,000 | ₹5,00,000 | +5% | +₹25,000 (+25%) |
| With 5x leverage | ₹1,00,000 | ₹5,00,000 | -5% | -₹25,000 (-25%) |

All figures are illustrative, not a forecast. The same 5% price move results in a much
larger percentage gain or loss on your own money, when leverage is used, in both
directions.

### Margin call

A **margin call** - a demand from the broker to deposit additional funds or securities,
when the value of your position falls enough that your existing margin no longer meets
the required minimum, to keep the position open.

1. The value of your leveraged position falls.
2. Your margin balance (relative to the position's current value) drops below the
   required minimum.
3. The broker issues a margin call, asking you to add funds or reduce the position.
4. If you do not respond in time, the broker may act without further individual
   confirmation, as described next.

### Auto square-off

**Auto square-off** - the automatic closing of a position by the broker, without further
individual confirmation from you, when required margin is not maintained, or when an
intraday position is not manually closed before the exchange's specified deadline.

::: warning 📅 Check the latest
Exact margin requirements, margin call procedures, and auto square-off timings and
charges differ by broker and by product, and are subject to SEBI and exchange rules that
can change. Confirm the current rules on your specific broker's website before trading
with leverage.
:::

::: tip 💡 Real-life example
Farah opens a leveraged intraday position worth ₹5,00,000, using ₹1,00,000 of her own
money as margin.

| Time | Event | Outcome (illustrative) |
|---|---|---|
| Morning | Position opened | ₹1,00,000 margin deposited, ₹5,00,000 position size |
| Midday | Price falls 3% | Loss of ₹15,000 on the position; margin balance drops |
| Afternoon | Margin falls below required minimum | Broker issues a margin call |
| Farah does not add funds | Auto square-off triggers | Position is closed automatically, locking in the loss |

This is illustrative, not a forecast. It shows how a relatively small price move against a
leveraged position can trigger consequences that happen quickly, and sometimes without
further individual warning beyond the initial margin call.
:::

::: warning ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Using leverage without understanding that losses, not just gains, are magnified equally.
- Assuming a broker will always give ample time to respond to a margin call.
- Not tracking margin levels actively during a trading day.
- Treating leverage as a way to "grow money faster" without accounting for the higher risk
  involved.
:::

::: danger 🚨 Safety check
Leverage substantially increases the risk of large, fast losses, including the possibility
of losing more than your original margin in some circumstances. Beginners are strongly
advised to fully understand margin requirements, margin calls, and auto square-off rules
before using any leveraged product. The specific mechanics of MTF, futures, and options,
where leverage commonly applies, are covered in the rest of this chapter.
:::

::: details 🧠 Go deeper (university level)
Hull's *Options, Futures and Other Derivatives* explains margin as a risk-management
mechanism used by exchanges and clearing corporations (introduced in Unit 2.1) to ensure
that leveraged positions remain adequately collateralised at all times, protecting the
broader market from cascading defaults. SEBI's margin framework for the Indian market
mandates minimum margin requirements and specifies how and when brokers must act on
shortfalls, referenced in NISM derivatives workbooks.
:::

## Quick recap

- Leverage magnifies both gains and losses, in percentage terms, compared to using only
  your own money.
- Margin is the deposit required to open and maintain a leveraged position.
- A margin call asks for additional funds when your margin falls below the required
  minimum.
- Auto square-off can close your position automatically, without further individual
  confirmation, if margin is not maintained.

## Check yourself

1. Why does leverage increase both potential gains and potential losses?
   ::: details Show answer
   Leverage lets you control a larger position with a smaller amount of your own money, so
   the same percentage price move translates into a much larger percentage gain or loss
   relative to your own invested amount.
   :::
2. What triggers a margin call?
   ::: details Show answer
   A margin call is triggered when the value of your position falls enough that your margin
   balance drops below the broker's required minimum.
   :::
3. What happens during auto square-off?
   ::: details Show answer
   The broker automatically closes your position, without further individual confirmation,
   typically because required margin was not maintained or an intraday position was not
   manually closed in time.
   :::
4. Why is it risky to use leverage without actively tracking your margin during the day?
   ::: details Show answer
   Because margin levels can fall quickly with price movements, and failing to respond to a
   margin call in time can lead to an automatic square-off, locking in a loss without further
   warning.
   :::
