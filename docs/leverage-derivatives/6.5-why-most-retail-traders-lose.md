## In plain words

Think of a video game arcade where most players spend more on tokens than they win back
in prizes, even though a few skilled or lucky players do walk away ahead. Data on
individual futures and options (F&O) trading in India shows a broadly similar pattern:
most individual participants lose money over time, even though some individuals do
profit.

## How it works

### What SEBI's studies have found

SEBI has published studies analysing the trading outcomes of individual investors in the
equity derivatives (F&O) segment. These studies have found that a large majority of
individual F&O traders incurred net losses over the multi-year periods studied, with
average losses per losing trader running into meaningful amounts of money, after
accounting for transaction costs.

### Why costs matter

Frequent trading, especially in derivatives, involves repeated transaction costs: brokerage,
Securities Transaction Tax (STT), exchange fees, and other charges, covered in full in
Unit 8.1. These costs apply on every trade, win or lose, and can meaningfully erode the
net outcome for active traders, even when individual trades look profitable before costs.

### Why psychology matters

Common behavioural patterns, explored more broadly as investor behaviour in Unit 7.4,
appear frequently in derivative trading losses:

1. **Overconfidence** - overestimating one's own ability to predict short-term price
   movements.
2. **Loss aversion** - holding onto losing positions too long, hoping for a recovery, while
   closing profitable positions too early.
3. **Chasing losses** - increasing position size after a loss, attempting to recover
   quickly, which often increases risk further.
4. **Herding** - following tips or trends from social media or messaging groups, without
   independent analysis.

### Recent SEBI rule changes

SEBI has introduced measures over recent years aimed at moderating retail participation
risk in derivatives, including changes to minimum contract sizes, the number of weekly
expiry products per exchange, and margin requirements.

contract value requirements, current weekly expiry rules, and current margin norms, on the
SEBI website, since these have been revised multiple times in recent years.]
:::

::: warning ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Believing that skill alone can reliably overcome the cost and structural disadvantages
  most individual F&O traders face.
- Increasing position size or frequency after a loss, rather than reassessing the
  approach.
- Following unverified tips or "guaranteed profit" claims from social media or messaging
  groups.
- Treating derivatives as a shortcut to wealth, rather than a specialised tool with
  substantial risk, mainly suited to hedging or experienced participants.
:::

::: danger 🚨 Safety check
SEBI's own published research indicates that most individual traders in the equity F&O
segment lose money over multi-year periods, after costs. Anyone considering derivatives
trading should treat this as a serious, well-documented risk, not a rare outcome. Claims of
consistent or guaranteed profits from F&O trading, especially from unregistered advisors
or tip-sellers, are a major warning sign, covered further in Chapter 9.
:::

::: details 🧠 Go deeper (university level)
NISM derivatives workbooks and SEBI's investor education material frame derivatives as
tools primarily designed for hedging and price discovery in the broader financial system,
rather than as a retail wealth-building product. Behavioural finance research, referenced
broadly in Bodie, Kane and Marcus's *Investments*, documents overconfidence and loss
aversion as persistent, well-studied biases that disproportionately affect frequent,
leveraged trading decisions, helping explain why aggregate outcomes for retail derivative
traders tend to be poor even when individual traders believe their own approach is sound.
:::

## Quick recap

- SEBI's own studies have found that a large majority of individual F&O traders lose money
  over multi-year periods, after costs.
- Frequent trading costs and behavioural biases like overconfidence and loss aversion
  both contribute to these losses.
- SEBI has introduced rule changes over time aimed at moderating retail derivatives risk.
- Simpler alternatives, such as SIP-based diversified investing, carry meaningfully lower
  risk for most individual investors.
