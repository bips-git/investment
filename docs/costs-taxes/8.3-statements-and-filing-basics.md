# Statements and Filing Basics

<Badge type="tip" text="Beginner" /> <Badge type="info" text="8 min read" />

::: info 🎯 What you will learn
- What a capital gains statement is and where to get one
- What AIS is and why it matters for filing your tax return
- What tax-loss harvesting means
- What records to keep for smooth, accurate tax filing
:::

## In plain words

Think of preparing for a school exam using a well-organised set of notes, gathered
throughout the year, rather than scrambling to recall everything the night before. Filing
taxes on investments works much better with the same approach: organised records gathered
throughout the year, rather than trying to reconstruct everything at the last moment.

## How it works

### Capital gains statement

A **capital gains statement** - a summary document, usually generated by a broker, RTA
(introduced in Unit 2.2), or platform, listing each sale of shares or mutual fund units
during a financial year, along with the corresponding purchase details, holding period,
and resulting capital gain or loss (introduced in Unit 8.2).

1. Most brokers and RTAs, including KFintech and CAMS, provide a capital gains statement,
   often downloadable directly from their platform or website.
2. Cross-check this statement against your own records of purchases and sales during the
   year.
3. Use the figures for short-term and long-term gains separately, since they are taxed
   differently, as covered in Unit 8.2.

### AIS

**AIS** (Annual Information Statement) - a comprehensive statement, available on the
Income Tax Department's e-filing portal, summarising various financial transactions
reported to the department by banks, brokers, AMCs, and other institutions, associated
with your PAN (introduced in Unit 2.3).

::: warning 📅 Check the latest
The exact scope of information reported in the AIS, and the process for accessing and
correcting it, can be updated by the Income Tax Department. Confirm the current process on
the official e-filing portal before relying on it for return preparation.
:::

1. Log in to the Income Tax Department's e-filing portal to access your AIS.
2. Review reported transactions, including capital gains, dividends, and interest income,
   against your own records.
3. If a discrepancy is found, use the portal's feedback mechanism to flag it, since AIS
   data is meant to be used to cross-check, not blindly replace, your own accurate records.

### Tax-loss harvesting

**Tax-loss harvesting** - the practice of selling investments that are currently at a
loss, before a specific date within the financial year, in order to realise a capital
loss that can be used to offset capital gains elsewhere, reducing overall tax liability,
within the rules set by tax law.

::: warning 📅 Check the latest
[VERIFY: check the Income Tax Department's current rules on capital loss set-off and
carry-forward, including which types of gains a specific type of loss can offset, and for
how many years a loss can be carried forward, since these rules are technical and specific.]
:::

::: danger 🚨 Safety check
Tax-loss harvesting should be based on a genuine investment decision, informed by a
correct understanding of current tax rules, not solely as a mechanical year-end exercise.
Selling a fundamentally sound long-term holding purely to book a small tax loss can
undermine your original investment plan, discussed in Unit 7.1. Consult a qualified tax
professional if considering this strategy.
:::

### Records to keep

| Record | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Contract notes (Unit 8.1) | Proof of each trade's price and charges |
| Capital gains statements | Summarised gains and losses for tax filing |
| Demat and mutual fund account statements (Units 2.2, 2.3) | Proof of holdings and transaction history |
| Bank statements | Proof of funds moving in and out for investments |
| Dividend and IDCW payout records | Proof of income received and any TDS deducted |
| Form 16 or Form 26AS, and AIS | Cross-checking against reported information |

Keeping these records organised, ideally digitally backed up, for at least the period
required under applicable tax law, generally makes filing smoother and provides evidence
if the Income Tax Department raises a query later.

::: tip 💡 Real-life example
Before filing her return, Ananya gathers her records systematically.

| Step | Action |
|---|---|
| 1 | Downloads her capital gains statement from her broker's platform |
| 2 | Logs in to the e-filing portal and reviews her AIS for the year |
| 3 | Cross-checks AIS entries against her own capital gains statement and bank records |
| 4 | Notes a mutual fund sold at a loss earlier in the year, to be reported and considered
    for offsetting against other gains, following current tax rules |
| 5 | Keeps all documents organised in a labelled folder for future reference |

This is illustrative, not a forecast or personalised tax advice. It shows a general,
organised approach to preparing investment-related information for tax filing.
:::

::: warning ⚠️ Common mistakes
- Relying solely on memory or scattered messages instead of organised records throughout
  the year.
- Not cross-checking AIS against your own capital gains statement, missing discrepancies.
- Selling a fundamentally good long-term investment purely for a small tax-loss benefit,
  without weighing the broader investment impact.
- Discarding records too early, before the period required under applicable tax law has
  passed.
:::

::: danger 🚨 Safety check
Discrepancies between AIS and your own records should be addressed proactively through the
official feedback mechanism on the e-filing portal, rather than ignored, since inaccurate
reporting can lead to tax notices or complications later.
:::

::: details 🧠 Go deeper (university level)
The Income Tax Department's AIS framework was introduced to improve transparency and
pre-fill accuracy in tax returns, drawing on data reported by financial institutions under
statutory reporting requirements. Provisions for carrying forward and setting off capital
losses are governed by specific sections of the Income Tax Act, 1961, referenced in
professional tax literature, and are subject to conditions on timely filing of the return
in which the loss is first claimed.
:::

## Quick recap

- A capital gains statement summarises gains and losses on sales during a financial year,
  usually available from your broker or RTA.
- AIS consolidates financial transactions reported to the tax department under your PAN,
  and should be cross-checked against your own records.
- Tax-loss harvesting realises losses to offset gains, but should follow current tax rules
  and fit your broader investment plan.
- Keeping organised records, including contract notes and statements, makes tax filing
  smoother and provides evidence if questions arise later.
